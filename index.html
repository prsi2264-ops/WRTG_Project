My Second Post

November 21, 2026

Hello Friends! Welcome back to my blog post. I’m sorry I missed last week’s post on Monday I was sick for what felt like an eternity. Nevertheless, I am back now! And today we are going to talk about procrastination. Yes, it’s that time of the year friends. I know you do it, everybody does it. It’s just about who hides it well. Right? Well maybe not. John Perry on “The Art of Procrastination” says people like Bill Gates probably aren’t plagued with the same disease. Perry argues that us procrastinators do things only when we have something even more important to do. He offers an intriguing solution of having something at the “top” of the list of things we must do so that we deliberately do everything else on that list. Others may suggest productivity apps on the phone. But maybe it’s not just a “productivity” problem. Is it a problem with stress? Some sort of malfunction with motivation perhaps? Surely many of us have already had this line of thought already. If some of us dig deeper we may find roots of perfectionism, of striving for doing something so well that there is no possible way we could get rejected. This is exactly what Matthew Haig explores in the “The Midnight Library.” Nora Seed (the narrator and main character of the novel) doesn’t do anything not because she can’t but rather because there are too many expectations for her to even start. She has potential of course–just as every one of us does–but she also has expectations tied to each of those potentials. In the weight of it all she ends up in the “Midnight Library,” a place between life and death where she gets to explore every possible life she could’ve had. In that exploration one somewhat unsurprisingly might find that no life comes without its flaws. Every idealized variation of perfection when realized becomes hopelessly imperfect. Because life is imperfect at least according to Haig. This interpretation explores how deeply perfection itself is intertwined with procrastination. Though wanting the perfect result is not the only reason why we don’t start things, it is certainly a reason.

So what? Perfection exists, and it’s not necessarily something we can forcefully turn off. Is it the ruminations in our mind that hold us back? Perhaps we are left immobile because moving is painful in the first place. In the sense that every step kind of hurts a little bit. A lot of people talk about “getting the ball rolling,” or “just start and it will feel easier.” For some of us that sounds like blasphemy because we know that each step forward has just as much weight as the first. It’s like the friction never got lifted. It’s as if we just must keep pushing forward with each step seemingly taking the same amount of energy as the first. In other words, sometimes just starting doesn’t make things easier. If this problem was purely rooted in perfection, then convincing ourselves that a horrible result–heck, any result–is enough must catapult us into action like a tightly wound spring. Yet we know from experience that procrastination is not that easy to overcome. We’ve tried to convince ourselves that it’s okay to fail, we’ve even begged for failure yet we still don’t do the task at hand.

At this point things may start to look bleak because we have yet to figure out how we can beat perfectionism. Maybe after a while we give up and just say “eh, I’ll do it tomorrow.” Okay, c’mon I had to have that joke in there somewhere, right friends?

We need a new angle in this now; perfection is clearly a factor in procrastination, but perfection is not the whole picture. On top of that we can’t really seem to easily solve the connection that perfection has to this whole mess, nor can we actually use this angle to any benefit. So, one might take a little detour into the Himalayas (or some other calm place in your mind) and try to work on this holistically through this abstract idea of “stress.” After all procrastination makes me stressed, so why not just try to eliminate that stress, right? Brilliant idea however for me this is not such an easy task my friends. Maybe it’s just me but a procrastinator taking up meditation sounds a little bit like a monk taking up CrossFit. It just doesn’t fit (pun intended). Some form of anxiety is at the root of procrastination and sitting there doing nothing brings up ideas of everything that I am supposed to be doing. However, during this detour I certainly did pick up better sleep habits. Which actually ended up lowering my stress more than any type of meditation ever did. Turns out I can’t think of all the things I’m supposed to be doing when I’m literally unconscious! Seriously though, get your sleep schedule consistent my friends, it really does help.

Alright, as we’ve trekked along this journey we’ve found that perfection has something to do with procrastination. We also know anxiety is related somehow too. This is satisfactory, but we may be hungry for deeper answers to deeper questions. So, we trace procrastination down to deeper roots: we trace it down to avoidance. Certainly, we avoid tasks but in more general terms we avoid emotions. We avoid unpleasant emotions specifically. Now at this point you might be thinking “duh, of course we avoid unpleasant emotions, that is why we avoid tasks.” In which case I would ask why exactly it is that we avoid these unpleasant emotions?

In “Notes from Underground” by Fyodor Dostoevsky the Underground Man more or less procrastinates life. He refuses to participate in the dance of life because he is afraid of being rejected. So, he recedes into a Romanticized world of his own where he is better than everyone else. Due to the world belittling him he decides to belittle the world back through his thoughts. He fights back in his ideas and actions. He doesn’t interact with others unless he has to, and in the times he does interact with others he automatically regrets it. In the one time someone does offer him love, he rejects the offer by treating that someone as beneath him. Through reflection it seems to me that the Underground Man refuses to participate with the world as a way to protect himself (I’m sure there are other possible interpretations). In other words the Underground Man procrastinates participating in the world because he sees the alternative as harming himself.

The Underground Man builds on the “obvious” conclusion that we procrastination to avoid unpleasant tasks. He adds a subtler nuance: we procrastinate to protect ourselves. In “My Year of Rest and Relaxation” by Ottessa Moshfegh the main character/narrator (her name isn’t given) does exactly that: she procrastinates to protect herself. Except her form of procrastinating life involves going to sleep for as long as possible through any means necessary including medications. As the book title suggests the narrator falls asleep as much as she can for a whole year (and slightly more). Since the narrator doesn’t have any problems with money, she decides that if she takes a whole year off (by just sleeping) then she will come out a renewed person by the end. In twisted fashion she pushes on the extreme of procrastinating harder and somehow does actually succeed in wanting to live life. Moshfegh through the novel seems to make an argument that this “paradoxical intention” (term borrowed from Viktor Frankl) somehow might end up helping. Now, of course this seems like a very dangerous to suggest more procrastination could cure procrastination. That is not what I’m saying. What I’m saying is that when we drop the “fight” we may actually preserve enough energy to find the solution. If we procrastinate anyway and our efforts of “pushing through it” are futile then why waste the energy? Sure, there might be blow back, sure we might miss a deadline here and there, but we could always go back to the old ways of “pushing through it” at any moment. Why not on some days stop pushing and start putting that energy into finding a solution for procrastination? I think this is the true argument Moshfegh is making: if we keep trying to “push” through life there is a huge leak of energy. Sometimes it is beneficial to take a step back and stop using the same failed tactics on the same problem.

Personally, I’ve found that in not forcing myself to do assignments I’ve freed up enough time to try to tackle the root of the problem. The only solution was always to do the task in some exposure therapy sort of way. The problem wasn’t that I didn’t know the solution; at the end of the day everybody has to “reprogram” their emotions to not fear doing work. The problem was the approach. I kept trying to push through the “reprogramming” and got nowhere. But you can’t force yourself to relax. Doing tasks for me was difficult because there was always emotion attached to it. So, the method was to detach those emotions; to somehow learn to relax in the face of the task. And in that we unlock the hardest part of this entire thing, how do we even relax in the first place? I’m not going to answer that because that is choice that has to be explored personally. However, I have found that in relaxing you almost block out the terrifying emotions that come with procrastination. So, I’ve actually started trying to reverse the emotional cycle of avoidance. If you run from something you are running towards some other thing. For example, in running away from working on a project one might run towards social media. In this case you are taking the negative emotions and trying to soothe them with social media. Well, you can reverse the roles. You can relax in the face of the project (or even “bottle” up those emotions) and then open up the social media app and force yourself to feel those “bottled” up emotions while using the app. Yes, this is deeply uncomfortable, but I’ve found that it makes doing the task easier. And interestingly using social media and deliberately bringing up negative emotions actually makes me want to run towards what I’m supposed to be doing (that is how I got started writing this blog post).

Ok, the previous paragraph is a little bit “experimental” as I’m still playing around with different “solutions.” But I do hope that I’ve laid out the progress of me trying to solve this whole “procrastination” thing. I know this is a lengthier post than usual, but I hope you guys enjoyed it and found some value in it. If you did make sure to leave a like and comment your thoughts. I will try my best to respond. Thank you guys for your love and support. I will see you all on the next post.
